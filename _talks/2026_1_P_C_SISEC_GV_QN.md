## Abstract

This study investigates how gender stereotypes shape perceptions of intimate partner violence (IPV), focusing on physical violence in heterosexual relationships. Although IPV is a form of gender-based violence, it is often perceived as less serious, and its interpretation varies according to the gender of those involved, which can contribute to the normalization and minimization of certain forms of aggression. To investigate these dynamics, we conducted a between-subjects survey in Italy (N = 402), where participants were randomly assigned to one of three experimental conditions based on real Italian newspaper headlines about an instance of intimate partner violence. Three versions of the vignette were used, differing only in the gender framing of the event: one with a male victim and female perpetrator, one with a female victim and male perpetrator, and a gender-neutral version of the same event. Respondents were asked to evaluate their perceptions of the IPV scenario across several dimensions, measured on a five-point Likert scale: perceived seriousness of the violence, emotional involvement, willingness to intervene, attribution of victim responsibility, justification of the aggressor and recognition of institutional responsibility. Results show that the genders of both the victim and the aggressor affect how intimate partner violence (IPV) is perceived: when the victim is male the violence tends to be perceived as less serious and respondents show less willingness to intervene. Interestingly, respondents attribute greater responsibility to male victims and tend to justify the aggressor’s behaviour more than in the other treatments. Respondents consider institutional intervention necessary regardless of the scenario and their personal characteristics. Gender of respondents significantly influences perceptions of the act: men tend to perceive violence as less serious, while women show a greater emotional involvement. The analysis also shows an implicit gender bias in the control group: when asked to identify the gender of the victim and the aggressor in a scenario without gender-explicit references, all identified the victim as a woman and the aggressor as a man. In light of the analysis conducted and the findings obtained, it can be argued that the relevance of this project is twofold: it offers a fresh perspective on the impact of gender stereotypes in shaping perceptions around IPV, exploring the Italian context which remains deeply understudied. Moreover, although further research is needed to have a better and comprehensive understanding of the phenomenon, this study offers an original lens for studying how gender biases affect the interpretation of violence. This is therefore a broad issue, which offers many points for further investigation, but above all raises questions about the role of gender stereotypes in how violence is experienced in contemporary society. It is precisely for this reason that pursuing this line of research is not only useful at a purely academic level but is necessary in order to have a profound reading of the present and help design future educational programs and awareness campaigns.
